GUIDESteam Japanese Localization

10 things Japanese players notice on your Steam page in the first 5 seconds

Checked against live Japanese Steam store pages this week. Game names are withheld; the Japanese text below is quoted exactly as it appears on the page.

  1. 01

    Screenshots come before your words

    Japanese Steam shoppers typically scan screenshots, then jump to recent reviews, then system requirements — the description is often the last thing read, and read fast. Your first two lines of text carry almost all the weight; assume paragraph three is never read.

  2. 02

    A translated heading is not a translated page

    On one page I checked this week, the section was titled 「ゲームについて」("About the Game") — and everything underneath it was still in English. A Japanese heading with English content under it reads worse than an all-English page, because it signals "we started and stopped."

  3. 03

    English words dropped into Japanese sentences stand out immediately

    On another page, the mechanic names "Boost" and "Power-Ups" were left in raw Latin letters inside otherwise Japanese sentences ("Boost を使って攻撃力を高め"). To a Japanese reader that reads as an unfinished draft — the fix is usually just katakana (ブースト), not a rewrite.

  4. 04

    Idioms translated word-for-word turn into nonsense

    "Have you been racking your brains trying to strategize?" became, on a live page, 「頭の痛みに戦略を絞ったのですか？」— which reads literally as "did you narrow your strategy into a headache?" The English idiom has no Japanese equivalent word-for-word; it needs a rewrite, not a translation.

  5. 05

    Mixing polite and plain speech reads as two different writers

    です/ます (polite) and だ/である (plain) can both be correct choices for a store page — but switching between them mid-paragraph is one of the fastest tells that nobody read the page end to end before publishing.

  6. 06

    Half-width punctuation next to Japanese text looks copy-pasted

    Japanese typography uses full-width ！？（） with its own spacing rules. A half-width "!" or "(" sitting directly next to Japanese characters is a small thing, but it's exactly the kind of small thing a native eye catches on the first pass and a non-native eye never does.

  7. 07

    Repetition a machine wouldn't notice, a person will

    One short paragraph I checked used the word 運 ("luck") five times across three consecutive sentences. Grammatically fine, sentence by sentence — but read together it feels like a first draft nobody trimmed. Reading your own translated copy out loud, in one sitting, catches this in a way that reading paragraph-by-paragraph doesn't.

  8. 08

    Reviews get read for tone, not just score

    Japanese players commonly skim the text of recent reviews, not just the percentage — so a store page that reads as unedited invites review comments about exactly that, which then become part of what the next visitor reads first.

  9. 09

    System requirements get checked before wishlisting, not after buying

    This isn't unique to Japan, but it means your Japanese system-requirements block deserves the same care as your English one — a requirements section that's still in English on an otherwise Japanese page is one more small "did anyone finish this" signal.

  10. 10

    One native read beats one more translation pass

    None of the examples above are wrong in the sense of grammar-checker-wrong — they're wrong in the sense that a native reader stops, rereads, and quietly loses a little trust. A single read-through by a native speaker, out loud, before launch, catches nearly everything on this list in under fifteen minutes.

FAQ

How do I know if my Steam page needs a native Japanese check?

If a heading is translated but the paragraph under it is still in English, or an English mechanic name like "Boost" is left inside an otherwise Japanese sentence, that alone is usually enough of a signal.

What's the difference between machine translation and a native check?

Machine translation usually gets the grammar right but misses idioms translated word-for-word, mixed politeness levels (です/ます vs だ/である), and repeated words that read as an unedited first draft. A native read catches those in one pass.

How fast can a Japanese Steam page be translated or checked?

A store page translation with a native check normally turns around within 24 hours. A quick check of a live Steam link, with no changes made, is much faster.
